# MIRA Demo Video — 2:30–3:00

## 0:00–0:15 — Problem
Show title screen.
Voiceover: “Healthcare records tell us what happened. They rarely explain when a person's health trajectory changed—or what was actually knowable at that time.”

## 0:15–0:35 — Open the case
Open the synthetic patient timeline.
Voiceover: “MIRA investigates a longitudinal record as a sequence of states, signals, decisions and outcomes.”

## 0:35–1:05 — Find turning points
Click **Investigate what changed**.
Show three candidate turning points.
Voiceover: “Instead of producing another summary, MIRA asks a narrower question: where did the direction of health materially change?”

## 1:05–1:35 — Hindsight firewall
Open a turning point and select **What was knowable then?**
Voiceover: “MIRA freezes the evidence at that historical moment. Later information is shown separately as evidence evolution, so hindsight does not become false certainty.”

## 1:35–2:00 — Reversal and recovery loop
Show improving → reversing trajectory.
Voiceover: “When an improving trajectory reverses, MIRA does not simply issue another alert. It investigates what changed and presents bounded next-step categories: return to something previously associated with improvement, check a worsening signal, or discuss the change with a clinician.”

## 2:00–2:25 — Verify
Advance the synthetic timeline.
Voiceover: “MIRA then re-measures the trajectory. Recovery is not declared until another measurement period supports a sustained change.”

## 2:25–2:45 — Differentiation
Show architecture card.
Voiceover: “The core invention is a trajectory representation, turning-point method, hindsight-aware evidence model, and outcome feedback loop—not a generic health chatbot.”

## 2:45–3:00 — Close
Voiceover: “MIRA: reconstruct the trajectory. Find the turning point. Understand what changed. See whether the trajectory changed again.”
